Executive overview

Leadership summary

Northstar depends on Microsoft 365, cloud file storage, online donations, payroll, accounting, a website platform, and an outsourced IT provider. The review identified five leadership priorities: confirm administrator and former-worker access, document backup and restoration responsibilities, establish a payment-change verification process, define incident coordination across leadership and providers, and govern staff use of public AI tools.

02

Prioritized observations

Priority findings

Address FirstAddress SoonPlannedProvider Clarification
F-01

Former-worker and volunteer access review is not consistently documented

Address First
Scenario
A former worker retains access to shared files, email, or a fundraising platform because removal depends on informal notification.
Potential effect
Unauthorized access, accidental disclosure, loss of donor or customer trust, and uncertainty during an investigation.
Evidence basis
Customer-stated; no complete access-removal record was provided.
Current responsibility
Operations notifies IT, but no single accountable owner confirms completion across all systems.
Recommended action
Create one departure checklist covering email, files, financial systems, websites, cloud applications, vendor portals, and physical devices.
Completion evidence
Approved checklist, named owner, completed test using a recent departure, and retained confirmation record.
F-03

Backup recovery responsibility has not been confirmed

Address First
Scenario
A ransomware event or administrator error makes shared files unavailable, but leadership does not know which cloud data is backed up or who restores it.
Potential effect
Delayed operations, lost records, uncertain recovery time, and avoidable provider confusion.
Evidence basis
Customer-stated. Provider responsibility not confirmed from available information.
Current responsibility
Shared assumption between operations and IT; no named recovery decision owner.
Recommended action
Request written confirmation of coverage, frequency, retention, restoration ownership, testing, and customer duties.
Completion evidence
Written response, named internal owner, restoration contact, and agreed verification step.
F-06

Public AI-tool use is not governed

Address Soon
Scenario
Staff or volunteers enter donor, customer, employee, financial, or internal information into public AI tools without an approved-use rule or clear vendor review.
Potential effect
Confidential information may be exposed, retained, reused, or incorporated into business decisions without adequate review.
Evidence basis
Customer-stated use of multiple AI tools; no approved-tool list, prohibited-data rule, or assigned owner was provided.
Current responsibility
Individual users choose tools; leadership and IT responsibilities are not defined.
Recommended action
Create an approved-tool list, define prohibited information, require human review for material outputs, and assign one owner for AI-use decisions and incidents.
Completion evidence
Approved guidance, named owner, staff communication, configured enterprise controls where available, and a review date.

04

Provider follow-up

Questions to take back to providers

  1. Which Microsoft 365, cloud-file, website, and endpoint systems are included in your backup service?
  2. Who initiates and approves a restoration, and what information must the customer provide?
  3. Which administrator accounts do you control, and how often are they reviewed?
  4. What systems and responsibilities are explicitly excluded from the current service?
  5. What notification and coordination support is included after suspected account compromise or ransomware?
  6. Which AI features or integrations are enabled in our licensed services, what data can they access, and which controls remain our responsibility?
